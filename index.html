Master thesis · KU Leuven · 2026–2027

Using large language models to generate Bayesian prior distributions for predictions of clinical trial outcomes

Plan last updated

Overview

Interim analyses can stop a failing trial early by computing the predictive probability of success (PPoS). Early on, when little data has accrued, the prior on the treatment effect dominates that number, so a bad prior gives a bad futility signal exactly when it matters most. Building good priors today means slow expert elicitation (e.g. SHELF) plus heavy documentation for regulators.

This thesis tests whether LLMs can produce priors that are accurate, well documented and regulator-ready, and quantifies what they do to futility detection.

Sub-goal 1

Accuracy

Benchmark LLM-generated priors against a gold-standard reference prior.

Sub-goal 2

Documentation

Can the LLM write a rationale, with real references, good enough to cut the manual documentation burden?

Sub-goal 3

Futility impact

How does the prior (LLM vs uninformative vs expert-elicited) change the performance of the group's futility prediction model?

The gap this thesis fills. LLM-elicited priors have been studied for regression and hierarchical models, but nobody has yet measured how an LLM prior changes a PPoS futility decision, which is where the prior matters most because n is small.

    Methodology

    The three sub-goals are three tests applied to the same LLM-generated prior. Only the third needs the group's futility model, so sub-goals 1 and 2 can start before access to it.

    Prior + rationalequantiles → fitted distribution, cited evidence

    SG1 accuracy vs gold standard SG2 references + expert review SG3 PPoS futility simulation

    1 · Gold-standard reference

    A published trial protocol that reports its own pre-specified priors; otherwise the outcome of an in-house BREATHE trial. Prefer several reference trials, and trials completed after the LLMs' knowledge cutoffs, so an "accurate" prior is not just recall.

    2 · Prompting strategies & models

    • Direct zero-shot instruction
    • Riegler et al. structured prompt (simulated literature review)
    • Chain-of-thought over the treatment mechanism
    • Self-consistency: sample k priors, pool as a mixture (AutoElicit-style)
    • Retrieval-grounded on PubMed / ClinicalTrials.gov
    • Simulated SHELF panel: several LLM "experts" + facilitator LLM

    Models: GPT, Gemini, Claude (+ an open-weight model if trial data cannot leave UZ Leuven). Elicit 5th/50th/95th percentiles, then fit the parametric family SHELF-style rather than asking for σ directly.

    3 · Rationale & hallucination detection

    Every prior comes with a written rationale and references. Check: does each reference exist (DOI / PMID / NCT resolves)? Does it support the claim it is cited for? Does this step catch unsupported priors before they are used?

    4 · Expert & regulatory review

    At least one expert statistician scores the documentation, independent of numeric accuracy, against a rubric aligned with the FDA draft guidance (Jan 2026): pre-specified, centre justified, width justified, borrowing / discounting explained, sensitivity discussed, reproducible.

    5 · Downstream impact on futility detection

    Integrate the best-performing strategy into the group's futility prediction model. On simulated truly-futile and truly-effective trials, compare LLM, uninformative and expert-elicited priors on stopping time, correct-stop rate and wrong-stop rate, including under prior–data conflict.

    Background notes

    Clinical trials & interim futility

    A trial randomises patients to a new treatment or a control, measures a pre-specified endpoint and tests whether the difference is larger than chance. The thesis lives in one moment: the interim analysis, where a data monitoring committee decides whether to continue.

    Interim quantityDefinitionRole of the prior
    Conditional powerP(final success) assuming θ equals one fixed valueNone; ignores uncertainty about θ
    Predictive probability (PPoS)Conditional power averaged over p(θ | interim data)Enters through the posterior; dominant early
    Posterior probabilityP(θ > threshold | data) nowDirect
    PPoS = ∫ P(success at final analysis | θ, D) · p(θ | D) dθ

    In practice by simulation: draw θ from the posterior, simulate the remaining patients, run the final analysis, count successes. Typical rule: stop for futility if PPoS < 5–15%. (Saville et al., 2014; Dmitrienko & Wang, 2006)

    Why the prior dominates early

    Normal outcome, prior mean μ0 worth n0 patients, observed mean x̄ from n patients:

    posterior mean = (n0·μ0 + n·x̄) / (n0 + n)

    With a prior worth 40 patients, an interim look at 20 patients puts two thirds of the weight on the prior; at 400 patients it is under 10%. For a Beta(a0, b0) prior on a response rate, a0 + b0 plays the role of n0. This is the effective sample size (Morita, Thall & Müller, 2008).

    Elicitation today (SHELF) & regulation (FDA 2026)

    SHELF: train experts, elicit private judgements (quantiles, tertiles, roulette), discuss, agree a consensus prior, write a full record (Dallow et al., 2018). Works well (Holzhauer et al., 2022) but needs trained experts and a facilitator per trial; that cost is what the thesis wants to cut. The R package SHELF fits distributions to elicited quantiles.

    FDA draft guidance Use of Bayesian Methodology in Clinical Trials of Drug and Biological Products (12 Jan 2026): priors pre-specified with written rationale; discounting and sensitivity analyses for prior–data conflict; operating characteristics by simulation; reproducibility (software, code, seeds). Early futility decisions are named as a use case.

    LLM priors: opportunities & difficulties

    Opportunities

    • Minutes instead of a multi-week workshop
    • Broad literature knowledge; converts OR / HR / rates on the fly
    • Writes the rationale regulators ask for
    • Ensembles of models / prompts act like an expert panel
    • Retrieval and tools ground claims in real evidence

    Difficulties

    • Calibration: too narrow or uselessly wide
    • Hallucinated or misattributed evidence
    • Memorisation / leakage of trial results
    • Non-determinism vs pre-specification
    • Prompt sensitivity and anchoring
    • Numeracy across effect scales
    • Publication-driven optimism, the wrong direction for futility
